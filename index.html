Who I Am

About Me

I'm a Mathematical Epidemiologist and Data Scientist specializing in statistical modeling, machine learning, and public health research. My work sits at the intersection of climate-health analytics, Bayesian spatio-temporal modeling (R-INLA, SPDE, GAMM), and full-stack software development turning complex environmental and epidemiological data into evidence for real decisions.

At KEMRI in Nairobi, I lead data engineering and spatial analysis on a malaria risk study spanning 47 Kenyan counties and 62,000+ surveillance records. I'm also the founder and CEO of SCEFI (Safe Childhood, Educated Futures Initiative), an NGO advancing child protection and education access in underserved rural districts of Rwanda.

I hold an MSc in Mathematical Sciences (Epidemiology) from AIMS Rwanda as a Mastercard Foundation Scholar, and a B.Sc. (Hons) in Mathematics and Statistics from the University of Rwanda. I combine rigorous mathematical thinking with modern computational tools to deliver insight from public health surveillance to financial markets.

Impact of Enhanced Seasonal Malaria Chemoprevention on Haemoglobin Trajectories: A Longitudinal RCT Analysis

Boosts child haemoglobin by 1.23 g/dL. Analyzed longitudinal RCT data (300 children, 1,200 observations) evaluating Enhanced Seasonal Malaria Chemoprevention with iron/nutritional supplementation against the Standard protocol over 6 months. Applied Linear Mixed Models (random intercept + slope) in R, achieving significant treatment×month interaction (β=0.205 g/dL/month, p<0.001) with ICC=0.704 justifying the multilevel approach.

R LMM Longitudinal RCT
